My First Year Living Alone in a New City

January 7, 2027 · 2 min read

At twenty-six, I moved out of my family's house and rented a small studio apartment in a city I had never lived in before.

In the beginning, everything felt new and exciting. There was the local diner where the owner quickly remembered my usual order, the riverside park just a ten-minute walk away, and a used-book market that only pops up on weekends. I signed up for a pottery class near my office and bonded with a classmate over several misshapen bowls. I even made a new friend at my favorite coffee shop after striking up a conversation about the book she was reading.

My days were full. I had places to go after work, plans for the weekend, and a fridge stocked with meals I was learning to cook for myself. I often thought about how wonderful living alone could actually be.

But occasionally, on certain very quiet nights, a thought would suddenly pop into my head: If I slipped in the bathroom, or if I woke up in the middle of the night with a high fever and couldn't get out of bed, who would notice? My new friends were great, but they wouldn't think to check on me every single day. My family loved me, but they were in another city and didn't know how my day-to-day life was going.

That was the first time I seriously opened ANLY. I added my parents, my sister, and my closest friend from pottery class as my Carers. It started as just a trial, a quick tap before going to sleep every night.

Over time, it slowly became a habit, as natural as brushing my teeth. That check-in isn't about reporting my whereabouts; it's just a way to let the people who care about me know that I'm doing fine today. And with that peace of mind, I could finally, little by little, turn this new city into my true home.
