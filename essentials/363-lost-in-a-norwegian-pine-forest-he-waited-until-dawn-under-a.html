Lost in a Norwegian Pine Forest, He Waited Until Dawn Under a Giant Tree

December 2, 2026 · 2 min read

David, a software engineer, was transferred by his company to Oslo, Norway, moving ahead on his own while his wife and two kids stayed back in Seattle.

With a nine-hour time difference, busy school and work schedules meant they video-called twice a week while juggling daily life apart. Before he left, his wife set up ANLY on his phone, adding herself, David's sister, his college best friend, and two Oslo coworkers—Ryan and Emily—as Carers. Every morning upon waking, checking in was the first thing David did.

During his first winter in Norway, he fell in love with cross-country skiing. That Saturday, after tapping check-in, he drove solo to a mountain trail, posting a photo of the trailhead map inside the family Room beforehand with a caption: "Skiing here today."

By afternoon, the sky darkened abruptly and heavy snow moved in. Taking a wrong turn at a fork, David fell on a steep descent and twisted his knee so badly he couldn't stand. There was zero cell reception in the woods, and the freezing cold quickly drained his phone battery.

Remembering his backcountry safety training, he stayed put instead of wandering, sheltering beneath a thick spruce tree. Putting on every extra layer from his backpack, he kept moving his hands and toes all night to stay awake.

Sunday morning, he missed his check-in.

By noon, all five Carers received an alert. It was the middle of the night in Seattle, so his wife hadn't seen it yet; his sister called with no answer.

Seeing the alert, Emily immediately opened the shared Room, spotted the trailhead map photo, and called the local police alongside Ryan to give Search and Rescue the exact trail name.

That afternoon, rescue teams located David near the trail. He suffered mild frostbite and a sprained knee ligament, needing a few weeks to recover.

When his wife woke up and saw the group chat, she cried for a long time. "I was fast asleep," she said later, "thank goodness someone else was awake."

Five people got the alert, and Emily and Ryan acted immediately. Having just one or two people notice is enough.
