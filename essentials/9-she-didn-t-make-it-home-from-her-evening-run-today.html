She Didn't Make It Home from Her Evening Run Today

December 21, 2026 · 2 min read

Sarah runs along the river trail every evening at 6:30. For nearly a year, rain or shine, she’s made it a habit to check in on ANLY right after she gets home and takes a shower. Her Carers include her good friend Emily who lives in the same neighborhood, her sister in San Diego, and her college roommate Jessica. They’re all used to seeing her check-in every night.

It got dark unusually early that day. As she reached the most isolated part of the trail, a dog suddenly darted out from the bushes. Startled, she lost her footing and tumbled down the grassy embankment. The moment her ankle hit the ground, the pain was so sharp she couldn't stand up. Her phone flew out of her armband and disappeared into the tall grass.

That section of the trail was usually empty, and it grew eerily quiet after dark. She tried shouting for help, but only the wind answered. Knowing she wouldn't be able to check in today, she hugged her knees, shivering as she waited.

Meanwhile, Sarah's Carers started receiving alerts that she had missed her check-in. Her sister called first, but no one answered. Jessica sent a text, which was left unread. Emily remembered Sarah's regular running route and decided to drive over to the trail to take a look, while the others kept trying to reach her.

Emily drove slowly along the road next to the trail, shining a flashlight down the embankment. Finally, she spotted Sarah huddled in the dark. She immediately called 911 for an ambulance and texted the others: "Found her. She's safe, but her ankle might be sprained."

Sarah often says now that the scariest part of that night wasn't the pain. It was the thought that if it weren't for that missed check-in, she probably would have been out there until the next morning before anyone noticed she was missing.
