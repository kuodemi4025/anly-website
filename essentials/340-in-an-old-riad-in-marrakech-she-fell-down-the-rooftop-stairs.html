In an Old Riad in Marrakech, She Fell Down the Rooftop Stairs

November 30, 2026 · 2 min read

Hannah is a freelance graphic designer who travels the world with just her laptop. This winter, she rented a traditional courtyard house in the old medina of Marrakech, Morocco, planning to stay for a month.

With a seven-hour time difference from her family—her parents in Austin and her younger brother in Houston—they usually video-called once a week at most. Before leaving, she added her parents, her brother, and two college best friends, Grace and Noah, as Carers. "Check-ins are free anyway, so adding a few extra people gives me peace of mind," she thought. Every morning, she checked in before brewing her coffee. She had also posted photos of the house and a screenshot of her booking in the family Room.

One evening, she climbed up to the rooftop terrace to stargaze. The old house's stairs were steep, narrow, and had no handrail. Heading back down, she missed a step and tumbled halfway down the flight.

Her ankle swelled immediately and her lower back slammed against the steps, leaving her unable to stand. Her phone sat on a lounge chair up on the roof, out of reach. Tucked deep down a quiet alley, nobody could hear her calls for help. She pulled a throw blanket around herself and lay at the foot of the stairs all night.

The next morning, she missed her check-in.

By noon, all five Carers received an alert. Her parents called repeatedly with no answer.

Remembering the booking screenshot in the Room, her brother immediately messaged the host through the rental platform, while Grace chimed in on the group chat: "I speak some French—let me call him directly."

Living nearby, the host arrived that afternoon with a spare key. Knocking at the front door, he heard Hannah shouting with all her strength inside, unlocked the door, and called a cab to take her to the hospital.

Doctors confirmed a severe ankle sprain and bruised lower back—nothing a few weeks of rest wouldn't heal.

"Five people got that alert, and my brother and Grace jumped right in," Hannah said later. "Having just one or two people notice is all it takes."

Now, she still taps check-in every morning before making her coffee.
