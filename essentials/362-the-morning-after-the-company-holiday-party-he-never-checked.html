The Morning After the Company Holiday Party, He Never Checked In

December 7, 2026 · 3 min read

After graduating from college, Tyler moved to Chicago on his own for work, attending his very first company holiday party this year.

He was close with his family back in Denver, though none of them were big on phone calls—talking once or twice a month was plenty. When he moved to Chicago, his mom insisted he install ANLY. He added his parents, older brother, college roommate Jake, and two coworkers—Sam and Maya—as Carers, making it a habit to check in every morning before leaving for work.

At the holiday party that night, drinks kept coming around the table. Tyler rarely drank, yet ended up having several glasses. A coworker called him a cab home, and he made it up to his apartment on his own.

Stepping inside, he headed to the bathroom to wash his face. Suddenly dizzy, his foot slipped on the tile; his forehead struck the edge of the sink, and he collapsed onto the bathroom floor.

The next morning was Saturday—and he missed his check-in.

By noon, all six Carers received an alert. His mom called several times with no answer. His brother texted the group chat: "He had his holiday party last night—probably still sleeping it off?"

Sam got the alert too. Remembering how unsteady Tyler looked getting into the cab the night before, and living just a few blocks away, Sam replied to the group: "I'm heading over to check on him."

Arriving at Tyler's building, Sam asked the building manager to come upstairs with him. After minutes of loud knocking, a faint groan came from inside. The manager contacted the landlord, who hurried over with a spare key.

They found Tyler sitting on the bathroom floor with a large bump on his forehead, still dizzy. Sam took him straight to the emergency room, where a doctor put in a few stitches and advised rest, telling him to come back if he felt unwell.

That afternoon on the phone, his mom didn't scold him—she simply said, "Next time you have a few drinks, tap check-in the moment you get home."

Six people got the alert, and Sam was the one who knocked on the door. Having just one person show up is all it takes.
